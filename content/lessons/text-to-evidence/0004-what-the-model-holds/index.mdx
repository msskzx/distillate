Making Siyar volumes 4 and 5 exact quotes
showed where the current model drifts. This lesson lays out how it is built, groups the defects by
cause, and tests the model against the works the project wants next: another sira, al-Bukhari,
tafsir and the tarajem. It names pressures and open questions. It does not propose the redesign.

## 1. How the model is built

### The stores, and what copies from what

A value the app shows passes through five stores. Two are authored by hand, two are derived, and
one is authored and derived at once.

<StoresDiagram />

1. **Page store.** One printed page of one edition per file, for example
`data/history/sources/siyar-alam-al-nubala-risalah/v4/41.md`, with the editor's
footnotes beside it in a `.notes.md`. A page belongs to its volume, not to the entry that
first needed it (<RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/adr/0018-a-page-belongs-to-the-edition.md">ADR 0018</RepoLink>).
2. **Batches.** `data/history/batches/<batch>/batch.json` holds source
accounts, claims and citations, plus an approval block
(<RepoLink repo="namaq" path="src/lib/history/batchSchema.ts" />).
3. **Catalog.** `data/catalog/{people,battles,events,utterances}/*.ts`, one
module per subject, holding the values the app shows (<RepoLink repo="namaq" path="src/lib/catalog/types.ts" />). These are
typed by hand. The header of <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="data/catalog/people/abu-ubaydah-ibn-al-jarrah.ts" /> says its
Arabic is verbatim from the batch, which is a promise the file makes about itself.
4. **PostgreSQL.** <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="prisma/schema.prisma" />: `Person`,
`PersonVirtue`, `HistoricalClaim`, `Citation`,
`SourcePage`, `SourcePassage`, `Utterance`. Written by
`history:import` (evidence) and `catalog:project` (values).
5. **Neo4j.** Nodes and relations projected from the catalog by
`catalog:project-graph`. Person-to-person relations have no PostgreSQL table
(<RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/adr/0010-author-historical-data-under-data.md">ADR 0010</RepoLink>).

Behind these sit the dormant seeds, `prisma/personSeedData*.ts` and
`neo4j/*Seed*.ts`. <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="AGENTS.md" /> calls them "a checklist, never a source", but
while a seed still declares a person the catalog only adds to that person. So for most subjects two
authoring paths are live at once (<RepoLink repo="namaq" path="AGENTS.md" />, Historical evidence data).

### The five nouns

**A claim** is a record in a batch: a `key`, a subject, either a
`field` or a relation, a free-text `assertion`, a `confidence`
(`ESTABLISHED`, `LIKELY`, `DISPUTED`, `UNASSESSED`) and a
`reviewStatus` (<RepoLink repo="namaq" path="src/lib/history/batchSchema.ts" />). A claim that names neither a
field nor a relation is rejected by `history:validate` (<RepoLink repo="namaq" path="AGENTS.md" />).

**A citation** hangs off a claim. It carries a `passageAnchor`, an
`excerptArabic`, a `volume` label, a `pageReference` and an
`extractionUrl` (`batchSchema.ts`; `Citation` in
`prisma/schema.prisma`). The excerpt is a second copy of the page's words.

**An anchor** is `<volume>/<printedPage>-p<n>`, where
*n* is the paragraph's position after splitting the page on blank lines.
`pageAnchors` in <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="src/lib/history/sourceStore.ts" /> computes it on read; nobody
authors it (<RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/adr/0018-a-page-belongs-to-the-edition.md">ADR 0018</RepoLink>).

**A catalog value** is a field such as `fullName` or a list entry such as
a virtue, with `claims: ['<claim key>']` or the marker
`legacy-unreviewed` (the `Provenance` type in
`src/lib/catalog/types.ts`). The value's text is typed separately from both the claim's
assertion and the citation's excerpt.

**An approval** is a hash. `batchRevision` hashes the whole
`batch.json` minus the approval block, and `checkApproval` compares it to the
recorded revision (<RepoLink repo="namaq" path="src/lib/history/batchSchema.ts" />). Approving for publication and
marking reviewed are separate acts, and neither implies the other
(<RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/adr/0008-separate-review-from-visibility.md">ADR 0008</RepoLink>, <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="AGENTS.md" />).

### Which relation decides what a profile shows

The chain is: value → claim key → claim in a batch approved at its current revision → citation
→ anchor → page. `approvedClaimKeys` in <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="scripts/data/validateCatalog.ts" />
collects keys only from batches whose `checkApproval` passes, and a catalog value whose key
is not in that set is an issue.

The profile shows the *catalog value*. It does not read `claim.assertion`, and
it does not read `citation.excerptArabic`. The key is a pointer that says "this value
rests on that claim". Nothing on the pointer checks that the value and the evidence say the same
words. That one fact explains most of section 2.

## 2. The drift and the defects

The counts below come from the session that made volumes 4 and 5 exact quotes, recorded in
<RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/plans/data-model/00-evidence.md" />. They are grouped by cause, not by date.

### Cause A: copies of the same text in several places

One sentence of al-Dhahabi can exist four times: in the page file, in
`citation.excerptArabic`, in `claim.assertion`, and in the catalog value. A
fifth copy lands in PostgreSQL. Each was typed or pasted separately.

- **What happened.** 25 PRs rewrote `assertion`, a field no profile value
  reads, before anyone saw that the profile reads the catalog. Of 727 catalog text values collected,
  414 failed an exact-quote check. `name`, `fullName` and `kunya` were
  unvowelled while the citation beside them was vowelled.
- **Where it shows.** `zubayr/full-name` in
  <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="data/history/batches/az-zubayr-ibn-al-awwam/batch.json" />: the assertion is unvowelled and
  ends with `القرشي الأسدي`, which the excerpt beside it does not contain.
- **What it cost.** Work spent on a copy that showed nowhere, and a profile that
  could disagree with its own citation while every check passed.

### Cause B: provenance as a long indirection, gated coarsely

- **What happened.** The path from a value to its words has five hops and a string
  key in the middle. Approval is one hash over the whole batch (`batchRevision`), so fixing
  one excerpt lapses every claim in that batch. Five batches carry no approval at all today
  (arwa-bint-abd-al-muttalib-siyar175, fatimah-bint-muhammad, qutaylah-bint-qais-al-kindiyyah,
  safiyyah-bint-huyayy, umm-shareek), and `catalog:validate` reports every value keyed to
  them.
- **Where it shows.** `checkApproval` in
  <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="src/lib/history/batchSchema.ts" />; `approvedClaimKeys` in
  <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="scripts/data/validateCatalog.ts" />.
- **What it cost.** A red `catalog:validate` on `main` becomes
  normal, and a normal red check stops being read. A correction becomes expensive, because it reopens a
  whole batch for publication.

### Cause C: anchors addressed by position

- **What happened.** `4/41-p3` means "the third blank-line block of the
  file". Re-splitting a page, adding a missing leading paragraph, or merging two lines re-points every
  citation after it, silently. Eight anchors pointed past the end of their page (for example
  `4/92-p15`). Lesson 0003 found Talhah's `4/23-p17` on a ten-paragraph page.
- **Where it shows.** `pageAnchors` in
  <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="src/lib/history/sourceStore.ts" /> splits on `/\n{2,}/` and numbers from 1.
- **What it cost.** An anchor proves only that a page has at least *n*
  paragraphs. Which words it points at depends on how the file was typed.

### Cause D: two numbering systems for one volume

- **What happened.** The anchor's prefix is the edition's own volume number. The
  citation's `volume` field is a free string. Across all batches it reads
  `"1"` 234 times, `"2"` 50, `"4"` 731, `"5"` 348,
  `"السيرة 1"` 523, `"السيرة 2"` 44. Shamela's `الجزء ١` is this
  edition's volume 4, so `"1"` and `"4"` name the same binding.
- **Where it shows.** `zubayr/full-name` again: anchor `4/41-p1`,
  `volume: "1"`. <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="AGENTS.md" /> (Content sources) and
  <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/adr/0018-a-page-belongs-to-the-edition.md">ADR 0018</RepoLink> both record that three
  accounts were misfiled by reading the host's label.
- **What it cost.** Misfiled accounts, and a field the reader cannot trust without
  re-deriving it from the anchor, which makes the field a copy with no job.

### Cause E: checks that verify something other than the rule

- **What happened.** <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="scripts/history/verifyExcerpts.ts" /> checks that an
  excerpt is a substring of the *one* paragraph its anchor names, after dropping footnote
  markers and collapsing whitespace. <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="AGENTS.md" /> tells authors to join fragments across page
  turns and paragraph boundaries and to mark omissions with an ellipsis. The verifier rejects exactly
  those. 41 of 105 batches failed it, with 248 FAIL lines. The verifier also says nothing about the
  catalog value, which is the text a reader sees. And it is not wired into
  `history:validate` ([`docs/lessons/NOTES.md`](/text-to-evidence/notes)).
- **Where it shows.** `zubayr/full-name` cites `4/41-p1`, but its
  excerpt runs on into p2 of <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="data/history/sources/siyar-alam-al-nubala-risalah/v4/41.md">v4/41.md</RepoLink> (`ابْنِ قُصَيِّ …` is its own paragraph).
  The citation is honest and the verifier fails it. The same normalisation is written twice, in
  `verifyExcerpts.ts` and as `normalizePassageText` in
  <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="src/lib/history/sectionHeadings.ts" />.
- **What it cost.** Agent fixes were accepted by their own checks while a reviewer
  found meaning changes in about 65 of about 300 values: dropped clauses, a changed speaker, a lineage
  link added or lost. A check that compares the wrong pair passes the wrong edit
  ([lesson 0003](/text-to-evidence/0003-a-check-that-exists-but-doesnt-run)).

### Cause F: untyped narrative fields that cannot say who is speaking

- **What happened.** `virtues` was one string joined from up to six
  claims (Hatib), so al-Dhahabi's narration and a companion's testimony read as one voice. Summaries
  were stitched from several places, such as Sa'd ibn Ubadah's Badr summary, which nobody wrote.
- **Where it shows.** Fixed for virtues only:
  <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/adr/0020-a-virtue-is-one-entry-with-one-speaker.md">ADR 0020</RepoLink> made a virtue a list
  entry with a `speaker` (`CatalogVirtue` in `src/lib/catalog/types.ts`,
  `PersonVirtue` in the schema). `appearance`, participation
  `summary` and the others are still single strings. The Abu Ubaydah module notes its
  `appearance` is "composed from 7-p9 and 23-p6".
- **What it cost.** Words put in the wrong mouth, which is the gravest error the
  owner names. ADR 0020 also bans `…` stitching for a virtue, while `AGENTS.md`
  still allows ellipses in citations, so two rules now disagree on what one quote may be.

### Cause G: edition differences

- **What happened.** Shamela serves this edition's sira volumes 1 and 2 bare, with no
  tashkeel (vowel marks) and no footnotes (`shamela.ws/book/10906/428`), while volumes 4 and 5
  are vowelled with footnotes. Some batches carried unvowelled islamweb text while claiming the Shamela
  edition.
- **Where it shows.** `HistoricalSource` and `SourceVolume` in
  `prisma/schema.prisma` know one edition per source slug. Nothing records which host
  rendering a page was taken from, beyond `extractionUrl`.
- **What it cost.** An exact-quote rule cannot hold when the "exact" text depends on
  the host. A quote matches its page or not according to which rendering was typed into the page.

## 3. Why it happened

Each cause above traces to one design property, not to a careless agent.

- **A: text is stored by value, not by reference.** A value, an assertion and an
  excerpt are each a string that *should* equal a span of the page. None of them *is* that
  span. Every copy is a place to drift, and <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/adr/0010-author-historical-data-under-data.md">ADR 0010</RepoLink>
  separated "what the work says" from "what Namaq selects" without saying the selection must be a
  pointer.
- **B: provenance is keyed, not structural, and the unit of approval is the file.**
  The claim key is a name both sides agree to. The approval covers a revision of a file, so its grain is
  whatever an author put in one file, not one value
  (<RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/adr/0010-author-historical-data-under-data.md">ADR 0010</RepoLink>: "Review batches remain
  revisioned approval units").
- **C: identity is derived from layout.** ADR 0018 made the anchor derived so it
  cannot drift from an authored list. It gained consistency and kept dependence on how blank lines fall
  in a file. A paragraph has no identity of its own.
- **D: the same fact has two fields with no owner.** The volume lives in the anchor
  and again in a free string. Neither is computed from the other.
- **E: checks test shape, not derivation.** The rule ("the value holds exactly as the
  source said it") spans value, citation and page. Each check looks at one pair, and the pair the
  reader sees, value against page, has no check at all.
- **F: narrative is a scalar.** A `String` column has no place for a
  speaker, a span boundary or an isnad. ADR 0020 fixed one field by making it a list of typed entries.
  The property remains for the rest.
- **G: a source is identified by work and publisher, not by witness.** The model has
  no layer between "edition" and "page text" to hold that two hosts render one edition differently.

## 4. Future works, one at a time

Each verdict names the model element that decides it. "Holds" means the current types take the
work with no new concept. "Strains" means it fits by bending a rule or overloading a field. "Breaks"
means a decision already recorded must be reversed.

### (a) Another sira or companion history: Ibn Hisham, Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat. Verdict: strains

Several accounts of one subject are already allowed. `SourceAccount` is unique on
`(sourceId, subjectKind, subjectSlug)`, so each work gets its own account of al-Zubayr
(<RepoLink repo="namaq" path="prisma/schema.prisma" />). Competing reports already become separate claims where the model
holds the value (<RepoLink repo="namaq" path="AGENTS.md" />; `DISPUTED` in `batchSchema.ts`).

The strain is in four places.

- **One account per subject per work.** The same unique key forbids a work that tells
  of a subject in two separate places, which Ibn Sa'd does when a companion appears under a tribe and
  again elsewhere. The work would need a second slug or a merged span.
- **Choosing between works.** <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/adr/0010-author-historical-data-under-data.md">ADR 0010</RepoLink>
  says version one uses "one eligible historical source per focal subject and does not rank claims".
  A second work over the same subject is outside version one by its own text.
- **Numbering.** The anchor assumes volume and printed page. That holds for Ibn Sa'd
  in print. The free-text `volume` label (cause D) would add a third numbering system.
- **Variant names and identification.** A subject is a slug, and identity across
  works is whatever the author of the batch decided. Nothing records "Ibn Hisham's X is the Siyar's Y"
  as a cited, disputable claim. An event told differently by two works lands in two batches, and the
  catalog `summary` on a participation is one string with one wording.

**Decider:** ADR 0010's one-source-per-subject rule and the account's unique key.

### (b) Sahih al-Bukhari. Verdict: breaks

A hadith collection is the case <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/adr/0015-one-record-for-what-someone-said.md">ADR 0015</RepoLink>
wrote down in advance: "A hadith record wants an isnad, a collection, a book and a number, and a
grading vocabulary". It says every one of those "either contradicts a decision this repository
already made or asserts something it has no source for".

- **The isnad (chain of narrators).** Each link is a narrator and a mode of
  transmission: haddathana (he told us), akhbarana (he informed us), an (from), qala (he said). The
  model has no record for a link. `Utterance` has one `speaker` and one
  `speakerName` (`prisma/schema.prisma`), so a chain could only live as page
  text.
- **The matn (the wording), exactly.** `Utterance.textArabic` is a copy
  (cause A). For hadith the owner's bar is exact wording traceable to its reference, which a typed copy
  cannot prove.
- **One hadith, many places.** Al-Bukhari repeats a report across chapters with
  different chains and wordings. The model has no notion of two records being "the same report". Each
  would be a separate utterance, or the repetitions would be lost.
- **Muallaq reports.** A report with its chain cut at the start is a chain with a
  recorded gap. With no chain model there is no place for the gap either.
- **The book's own numbering.** Kitab, bab and hadith number are not volume and
  printed page. The anchor (`sourceStore.ts`) and `SourceAccountSpan` know only
  the latter. Different numbering editions of al-Bukhari would make cause D worse.
- **Grading.** `Utterance.grading` is free text in the source's own words
  (ADR 0015). Inside al-Bukhari grading is mostly the collection itself. Later graders are other works
  with their own claims.
- **Narrators who are already people.** Abu Hurayrah, Aisha and al-Zubayr are catalog
  people with Siyar biographies. Under the current rule their role as narrators can never be an edge.

**Decider:** the AGENTS.md rule keeping chains in source text, and ADR 0015's "not a
hadith model".

### (c) Tafsir and explanation books. Verdict: strains

- **Anchoring to a verse.** `Ayah` and `Surah` exist
  (`prisma/schema.prisma`), and the catalog already links people to ayat with claims
  (`AGENTS.md`: titles, Qur'an links). So "this explanation is about 2:255" has a target. A
  word within a verse has none: `Ayah.text` is one string.
- **The verse text.** It must come from the Qur'an table, not from the tafsir's own
  printing. The page store would hold the tafsir's copy of the verse inside its paragraph. Two copies of
  the most sensitive text in the system, and cause A says they will drift unless one points at the
  other.
- **Opinions attributed to Companions and Followers.** "Ibn Abbas said …" is an
  utterance with a speaker, which ADR 0015 covers. "with a chain" or "without" is the isnad problem of
  (b) again, in smaller form.
- **Disagreement side by side.** The model keeps competing accounts only "where the
  model holds the value they compete over" (`AGENTS.md`). An explanation of a verse is not
  a value the model holds today. Either it becomes one, or the disagreement stays in page text and is
  not structured at all.

**Decider:** no model value for "explanation of a verse"; the claim rule admits only
fields and relations.

### (d) Tarajem, biographical dictionaries. Verdict: strains, close to breaking

- **One person across many works.** This is what the account model was built for,
  apart from ADR 0010's one-source rule (see (a)).
- **Identification disputes.** <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/data-pipelines.md" /> already says that
  settling a contested صحبة (companionship) "wants الإصابة" and that until it is extracted the contest
  is recorded, not resolved. There is no record type for "these two entries are one person" as a cited
  claim, so the identity of a slug is asserted, never evidenced.
- **Narrators as entities.** A tarjama (biography) in تقريب التهذيب exists because
  its subject is a narrator. Most of them would have no relation except teacher and student.
- **Teacher and student relations.** These are person-to-person relations, which the
  catalog and Neo4j can hold. But a relation that exists only because one narrated from the other is
  exactly what the current rule forbids. `docs/data-pipelines.md` also leaves the narrator's
  tabaqa (generation) as "open and not decided".

**Decider:** the narrator rule, and the lack of a cited identity claim.

### What the narrator rule does under (b) to (d)

The rule is in `AGENTS.md` and `docs/data-pipelines.md`: "Transmission chains
stay in the source text. A person mentioned only as a narrator does not become a graph node or an
edge." <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/adr/0015-one-record-for-what-someone-said.md">ADR 0015</RepoLink> reuses it to keep
poets out of the graph.

- Under **(b)** it rules out the core of the work. A hadith collection is, structurally,
  its chains.
- Under **(c)** it costs less. Most tafsir attributions name one speaker, which an
  utterance already holds. Chains in tafsir by transmission (al-Tabari) hit the (b) wall.
- Under **(d)** it removes the reason most entries exist. A narrator who is "only a
  narrator" in the Siyar is the subject in a tarajem work, so the rule's own condition stops being
  true.

The rule was written for a biography source where chains are support, not subject. It names a
role ("mentioned only as a narrator"), and each new work changes who has that role.

## 5. The future

### Pressure points, soonest first

1. **Exact quotes for what is already extracted.** Causes A, C and E bite now, on the
   Siyar alone. Every value fixed by hand can drift again, and the check that sees value against page
   does not exist.
2. **Approval grain.** Each correction lapses a whole batch (cause B). As exact-quote
   work touches all 105 batches, the cost grows with every pass.
3. **Volume and edition identity.** Cause D and G bite the moment the sira volumes are
   re-extracted with vowels, and again for any second work.
4. **Remaining scalar narrative fields.** `appearance`, participation
   `summary` and similar fields still cannot carry a speaker (cause F). They bite whenever a
   second voice enters a field.
5. **A second biographical source.** ADR 0010's one-source rule and the missing
   identity claim bite with Ibn Sa'd or Ibn Hisham.
6. **Tarajem and narrators.** The narrator rule bites with the first narrator-centred
   work.
7. **Hadith.** Al-Bukhari needs chains, transmission modes, report identity and its
   own numbering at once. It bites last and hardest, and it is where the owner's bar is highest.

### Rules and ADRs that would have to change

- <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/adr/0010-author-historical-data-under-data.md">ADR 0010</RepoLink>: one source per focal
  subject; batches as the approval unit; two authored layers whose link is a key.
- <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/adr/0015-one-record-for-what-someone-said.md">ADR 0015</RepoLink>: "not a hadith model"
  and its reuse of the narrator rule.
- <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/adr/0018-a-page-belongs-to-the-edition.md">ADR 0018</RepoLink>: anchors derived from
  paragraph position.
- `AGENTS.md` and `docs/data-pipelines.md`: "Transmission chains stay in the
  source text"; "author a claim only when it backs a value the model holds today"; competing accounts
  only where the model holds the value; ellipses allowed in citations, against
  <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/adr/0020-a-virtue-is-one-entry-with-one-speaker.md">ADR 0020</RepoLink>'s ban.
- <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/adr/0020-a-virtue-is-one-entry-with-one-speaker.md">ADR 0020</RepoLink> as a pattern:
  whether every narrative field gets the same treatment.
- <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/adr/0011-classify-the-role-of-cited-evidence.md">ADR 0011</RepoLink>: its evidence role
  on a citation would need to say whether a hadith or opinion came with a chain.

<RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/adr/0008-separate-review-from-visibility.md">ADR 0008</RepoLink> (review separate from
visibility) and <RepoLink repo="namaq" path="docs/adr/0009-citations-independent-of-profiles.md">ADR 0009</RepoLink> (citations
independent of profiles) survive every scenario above. They are the parts of the model that already
generalise.

### Questions the plan stage must answer

- Should a displayed value be a reference to a span of the page rather than a copy of it, and what
  is a span when it crosses paragraphs and pages?
- What gives a paragraph, or a span, an identity that survives re-splitting a page?
- What is the unit of approval: a file, a claim, or a value?
- Is `claim.assertion` needed at all, if no value reads it?
- Who owns the volume number, and does the citation keep any volume field of its own?
- What is an edition versus a host rendering, and can two renderings of one edition coexist?
- Which check compares the displayed value against the page, and does it run in
  `history:validate`?
- Does every narrative field become a list of single-speaker entries, as ADR 0020 did for virtues?
- Are ellipses allowed inside a quote, or is every quote one contiguous span?
- How does a cited identity claim ("this entry is that person") look, and who may dispute it?
- When does a narrator become an entity, and does that depend on the work or on the person?
- What is a transmission link: narrator, mode of hearing, and what else?
- How is "the same report" across chapters, chains and wordings represented, if at all?
- How does a work's own numbering (kitab, bab, hadith number) sit beside volume and printed page?
- How is a verse, or a word in a verse, the target of an explanation, with the verse text taken
  only from the Qur'an table?
- Which grading is a quotation from the work, and which is another work's claim about it?
- How does ADR 0010's single-source rule give way when two works disagree about one value?
