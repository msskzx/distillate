## World-models foundation

### Learned

A world model is an internal simulator, usually operating in a learned latent state rather than raw observations. Its value is not perfect realism; it is sufficiently accurate prediction of consequences for the agent's decisions. Planning can happen by searching or optimizing through imagined trajectories.

### Key distinction

MuZero demonstrates that a planning model need not reconstruct the world. It can learn only the representation, dynamics, reward, value, and policy signals needed by its planner.

### Open questions

How do latent dynamics models preserve long-term information? When should an agent trust imagination versus collect more real data?

## Dream-RSI replay worlds

### Learned

Dream-RSI uses a recorded discovery tree as an empirical world: alternative exploration policies can traverse different stored branches and receive already-recorded outcomes without rerunning the expensive agent or evaluator. The improved policy is then deployed online, generating new trees for later replay.

### Key distinction

Unlike Dreamer, which learns a parametric latent dynamics model, Dream-RSI replays a finite realized search space. Its simulator is grounded but cannot predict outcomes outside the recorded tree.

### Open question

How should a system detect when replay-policy improvements are overfitting historical trees and need fresh online exploration?
