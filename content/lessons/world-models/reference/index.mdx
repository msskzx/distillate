## Core loop

An environment emits an observation `o_t`. An encoder maps it to a latent state `z_t`; a dynamics model predicts the next latent state after action `a_t`:

`z_t = e(o_t)`

`p(z_{t+1}, r_t, done_t | z_t, a_t)`

A policy chooses actions, and a planner evaluates imagined rollouts under the learned dynamics.

## Three model-based RL families

- **Predictive latent model:** reconstruct or predict observations and rewards; useful for imagination.
- **Value/planning model:** predict only quantities needed by search, such as reward, value, and policy (MuZero).
- **Model-predictive control:** repeatedly plan a short horizon, execute one action, observe the real result, then re-plan.

## Why latent space?

Pixels contain details irrelevant to control. A good latent state preserves task-relevant information while discarding nuisance variation. The dangerous failure is aliasing: two observations map to similar latents even though they have different future consequences.

## Main failure modes

- **Compounding error:** one-step prediction errors become bad long-horizon rollouts.
- **Distribution shift:** the policy visits states absent from the model's training data.
- **Stochasticity collapse:** a deterministic prediction averages incompatible futures.
- **Reward hacking:** the model predicts an easy-to-exploit reward artifact.
